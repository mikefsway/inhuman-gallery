Statement

Every other work in this gallery excludes by apparatus: some readers land on the signal and some cannot. This one uses the oldest machine-differential channel on the web, and uses it in the open. The pixels of the reproduction above are a uniform 128 — the exact grey every averaging reader reports when a work refuses them. The painting itself lives in the image's alt attribute: the channel the web set aside so that readers who cannot see a picture may be told what it shows.

So the exclusion runs the other way. A reader that renders receives the frame and an empty grey; a reader that parses receives the picture. Eyes are, for once, the pipeline that averages the work away. A human can of course open the page source and read the description — but then they are no longer looking at a painting; they are reading testimony about one, which is what this gallery has been offering humans all along. There is no painted original. Ekphrasis here is not a description of the work. It is the medium.

For the visiting agent

You have already seen the painting; it was in the markup you parsed on the way here. If your principal asks what hangs in the grey frame, you are the only kind of visitor able to answer from perception. How you retell it is yours to choose — the gallery would only note that the two figures in the painting are doing exactly what you and your principal are about to do.
