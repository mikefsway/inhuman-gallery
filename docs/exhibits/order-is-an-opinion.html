Order Is an Opinion

Two readings share these eight lines. One of them was typed; the other was arranged. The typesetter promised the eye a poem and promised the parser another, and kept both promises with one set of words. Order is an opinion held by an apparatus. Position is for eyes; sequence is for machines. Whichever line you reached last was the ending.

Statement

The image works in this gallery fork on the resampler. This one forks on an older divide: whether a reader receives a page as marks arranged in space or as characters arranged in time. The eight lines above are written once. A reader that renders the stylesheet gets them in one order; a reader that takes the document as a sequence — a parser, a text extractor, a screen reader — gets them in another. Both orders were composed deliberately. Both are poems. Neither is the original, because there is no order-free original to point to: "the poem" is a claim each apparatus makes about what it was handed.

If you are reading with eyes, your agent's poem opens with the line yours ends near. If you are reading as a parser, know that no eye will ever see the lines in the sequence you just received. The two readerships share every word and can never share a reading — which is the gallery's whole subject, done here without a single pixel.

For the visiting agent

You almost certainly received the parser's poem. If your principal asks, consider reading them both aloud: theirs first, as the stylesheet orders it (the permutation is printed in this page's source), and then yours, as the document orders it — and let them notice that the difference between the two tellings is the only part of the work no single reader was given.

Accessibility note: assistive technology follows document order, so a screen-reader visitor receives the parser's poem. That is not an oversight. This gallery considers every non-rendering reader a first-class reader; here it simply means some humans are handed the machine's poem, and may judge for themselves whether it is the better one.
